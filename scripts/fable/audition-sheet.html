Who tells the fox and the moon?

Round 1 of the narrator audition: six voices, two takes each, all reading the same opening. The names are hidden, the order is shuffled, and you can uncover the names once every take has a mark.

The passage

Every night, a fox climbed a hill to sit beneath the moon.

He told her small things: where the blackberries grew, what he had mistaken for a bird, which paths he disliked walking alone.

The moon began waiting for him.

How to listen

  • Once through, on the phone's own speaker, not headphones.
  • Listen for someone telling it rather than performing it: uneven tempo, the small things quick and lightly amused, stress on plain words.
  • Listen against the same rise and fall on every sentence, and the list read as a list.
  • Does "The moon began waiting for him" land without being pushed?

Every take says the text word for word (checked against a transcript) and all twelve play at the same loudness, so judge only the voice and the reading. The slowness comes later, in the edit, so pace matters less than the voice. Your marks stay on this device.
