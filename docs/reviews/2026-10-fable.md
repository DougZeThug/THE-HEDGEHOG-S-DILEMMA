# The Fox and the Moon (the opening fable): review record

A narrated cut-paper theatre film that plays before the title on a first visit. Curtains part on a
small orange fox who climbs a hill each night to talk to the moon; they meet at a pond, where her
reflection can lie beside him; his hiding and her brightening wound each other; a branch he pulls
over the water for shelter hides her face; his late reach breaks only the reflection; separate
nights; and at the end he brings her a small saved story, and both look toward the pond. Black,
about three seconds of true silence, then *The Hedgehog's Dilemma*, then the room.

The artist's brief is "THE FOX AND THE MOON", package v2 (2026-10-23): the outcome brief
(`handoff/CLAUDE_CODE_START_HERE_v2.txt`), the production bible, the approved narration and the
orange reference. The package is kept locally in `references/The Fox the Moon/` (gitignored); the
narration and the reference are committed in `references/fable/`. The decisions below follow the
brief; the reviews and their outcomes are added as they happen.

## 1. Brief and audit (2026-10-31)

**Fixed by the brief.**
- Cinematic cut-paper theatre: tactile layered paper, visible cut edges, controlled depth,
  expressive light. Not silhouettes, anime, photoreal animals, 3D cartoon or motion graphics.
- The fox is a natural orange red fox (burnt-orange coat, cream chest and tail tip, dark socks,
  ear tips and nose), never a black silhouette, and stays orange in the dark.
- The moon is a faceless ivory paper disc; her feeling is placement, light, edge and timing.
- The approved narration (`references/fable/narration.txt`, 376 words, 33 paragraphs), read by one
  intimate, matter-of-fact narrator. No added moral.
- The branch is one act with two meanings: shelter for him, exclusion for her. Only the
  reflection breaks; the sky moon is never touched. His reach is regret, not capture.
- Unresolved ending, then black, true silence and the silent title, exactly *The Hedgehog's
  Dilemma*.
- Integrated without breaking the room: memories keep their no-dialogue rule; pause, skip, replay,
  captions and reduced motion stay available; nobody is trapped by a failed load.

**Free.** Tools, shot design, timing, sound and music decisions. The ten-shot storyboard and the
4:07 silent animatic show story order only and are not a timing target.

**The artist's decisions** (before this record):
- Spend up to **20,000 ElevenLabs credits through the connector**: audition, the artist listens
  and picks, then the full reading. Ledgers kept by hand.
- **The fable plays on a first visit**; returning visitors can watch it again.
- **Mirror the orange reference's look**, staged in Blender as 2.5D paper theatre, with the pieces
  cut from the reference and extended.
- **The final renders run on this PC, in EEVEE** (2026-10-31). Superseded after the bake-off: the
  compositor renders them (decision 1, 2026-11-01).

**Earlier decisions this brief overrides, as an explicit exception:** README:253 ("not a narrator
reading the walls") and the opening review's "no music in the opening"
(`docs/reviews/2026-10-cinematic-opening.md:18`). Both still hold for the room and the memories.

**The machine** (the artist's PC, 2026-10-31).
- Intel i5-9500 (6 cores), 32 GB RAM, **Intel UHD 630 integrated graphics only**.
- Blender 5.2.3 LTS headless. Unlike the cloud machine that made Almost, **EEVEE runs here**: a
  probe of twelve solidified paper cards, a glossy floor and two area lights rendered 1920×1080 at
  32 samples in about 10 s a frame (the first frame about 54 s, compiling shaders). Cycles runs on
  the CPU only.
- Python 3.12 (numpy, Pillow, OpenCV, SciPy, soundfile, torch on the CPU), ffmpeg 9.0.2 (VP9,
  H.264, EBU R128), Node 22, Playwright Chromium. The ElevenLabs connector supports `eleven_v4`.
- `npx tsc --noEmit` and all 580 vitest tests pass on this checkout.

**The narration's length.** 376 words: about 2:30 of speech at 150 words a minute, 2:55 at 130,
3:25 at 110. With the silences the bible asks for (after his request, after her obscured
reflection, after the last glance), the curtains, a held image of ease and the separate nights,
the film lands between about 3:15 and 4:00 before the title.

## 2. The proposal (for the approach review)

### Look: the orange reference, staged in Blender as 2.5D paper theatre (`scripts/fable/`)

**Cutting the art from the reference** (`cut.py`, `extend.py`; pieces to `references/fable/`).
- The reference is six panels of about 500×500 px. They are split and upscaled once with
  `scripts/upscale.py` (Real-ESRGAN, anime 6B model, on the CPU).
- **The fox** is cut into the articulated pieces the reference already shows at its split pins:
  head with muzzle, two ears, neck and chest, torso, hips, a tail in three segments, upper and
  lower legs (four each), paws. Each piece comes from the panel that shows it best (lying fox in 2,
  crouch in 3, close-up in 4, reach in 5), with its matte cleaned along a deliberate cut edge.
- **Extending:** edges hidden by overlaps are painted in from a coat texture built from patches of
  the fox's own fur; each piece's colour is matched across panels so he holds one burnt-orange coat,
  cream and dark markings. Poses the reference lacks (walking, curled asleep, telling, turning to
  the pond) come from re-posing the same pieces, never from redrawing him.
- **Scenery:** the moon's paper disc and glow (panels 1 and 6), the hill, the pond bank and stones,
  the tree band and distant hills, reeds and grass as separate stalks, lily pads, the leafy branch
  (panel 4) with each leaf a separate piece, the dark curtains (1 and 6), and paper grain for the
  indigo sky. What the reference doesn't hold at a usable size is extended in its manner (the far
  side of the pond, a path beneath the trees, leaves rearranged for the overhead view).

**The stage** (`stage.py`, following `scripts/almost/`'s structure: set, puppet, scene, xsheet,
render, assemble, checks).
- Each piece is a card with image texture and alpha, solidified to paper thickness; the cut edge
  shows the paper's core with a slight bevel to catch the key; a fine bump from the paper grain.
  Brass split pins at the joints, as in the reference.
- **The rig:** one armature with pivots at the pins. Joints turn in the card's plane with a small
  out-of-plane lean for depth, as a rod puppet is worked.
- **The moon:** an irregular ivory paper disc lit from behind so it glows through its fibres, with
  a soft halo card.
- **The water:** a dark, slightly glossy plane that reflects the real stage (an EEVEE planar
  probe), so the reflection is spatial; a separate reflection disc lies just beneath its surface.
  At the rupture only that disc and the water's own reflection break into horizontal strips that
  drift apart and settle. The sky moon is a different object and is never touched.
- **Light:** one raking moon key placed per shot, an indigo fill and a small amber accent. The fox
  keeps his orange in the dark by rim and separation, not a spotlight. "Too much light" is a soft
  shaft through haze cards, as in panel 3.
- **The camera:** a proscenium, mostly locked off; slow pushes and parallax only where the story
  needs them. Each shot framed twice: 16:9 for screens, 4:5 for phones held upright. The branch is
  one act seen twice: at bank level for him, then from above for her (panel 4), with one rustle
  continuing across the cut.

**Acting** (`poses.py`, `xsheet.py`). Key poses: sitting and looking up; telling (head tilt, an
ear flicked); walking; lying loose by the reflection (panel 2); closing inward under the reeds;
trembling, on ones; relief; tightening; drawing the branch over (panel 4); the reach with an open,
regretful paw (panel 5); asleep under shelter; sitting and turning toward the pond. On twos at
24 fps, holds are real holds, and breath, ears and tail each run on their own clock. The exposure
sheet is keyed to the narration's word timings.

**Rendering** (`render.py`). `--draft` at half size and low samples for every review; held frames
rendered once; finals in EEVEE on this PC (estimated 8–16 hours for both framings, overnight).
Frames go through ffmpeg to VP9 and H.264, wide and tall, each with an `@low` copy, and a poster.
Review aids before any full render: a sheet of the cut pieces, a pose sheet, and lookdev stills
beside the matching panel of the reference.

### Narration (the ElevenLabs connector, `eleven_v4`, cap 20,000 credits)

- **Search:** narrative, calm, soft, mature English voices, male and female, from the voice
  library, plus the repo's own shortlist; none of the room's voices, so the narrator stays apart.
- **Audition** (about 3–4k credits): about six voices, two takes each, of two passages ("The moon
  began waiting for him. / From so far away…" and "But now he could see the whole of himself…").
  Sparse v4 tags only where delivery should change. Every take transcribed and checked against the
  text, then put on the audition sheet (`scripts/audition.mjs` gets a fable group). **The artist
  listens and picks**; picture work continues meanwhile.
- **Full reading** (about 6–8k credits): ten paragraph groups, two takes each, directed per the
  bible ("remembered rather than performed"; "The moon began waiting for him" lands; silences after
  his request, her obscured reflection and the last glance). Weak lines retaken within the cap;
  approved takes never overwritten.
- **Timing:** word timings from the chosen takes drive the exposure sheet and the captions
  (`public/fable/fable.vtt`, `src/content/fable-captions.json`), whose text must match
  `narration.txt` exactly.

### Sound and music (`scripts/fable/sound.py`, stems kept)

- **Material sound made in code:** curtain cloth; night air and grass; soft paws; water lapping;
  one continuous leaf-and-wood rustle carried across the branch cut; a single small wet touch, then
  ripples fading (never glass, never an impact).
- **Music:** ElevenLabs Music's terms exclude film, so the score is local: the FreePats Upright
  Piano KW (CC0, as in Things) and a bowed tone. One shared phrase in their company; incomplete in
  "darkness briefly"; it falls away at the branch, is absent in the separate nights, and never
  resolves.
- **Mix:** dialogue first and intelligible on a phone speaker; no startling peaks; about −16 LUFS
  integrated; stems for dialogue, music and effects. The soundtrack is muxed into the film files,
  so sync is native and the browser streams it.

### Integration (`src/fable/`, small changes in `App.ts`)

- **`FablePlayer.ts`:** a full-screen `#fable` layer with a `<video playsinline>`; wide or tall by
  aspect, `@low` on touch or low quality. Its audio goes through `createMediaElementSource` into the
  AudioEngine, after pause and the limiter, so volume, mute and pause apply. `play()` runs inside
  the `enter()` tap (`src/App.ts:354`), so it may start with sound.
- **Controls:** Skip (the existing `#skip`, or Esc) goes to black and silence, then the title.
  Pause and resume by tapping the film, or Space or K, with a visible state; a hidden tab pauses
  instead of skipping. Captions by a CC button or C, following the captions preference and raised
  above the fable layer.
- **Reduced motion:** no flashes; Skip focused; the camera question is open (question 5 below).
- **Failure:** if the film can't start within 8 s, or stalls for more than 6 s, straight to the
  title (with a console warning).
- **`fableScore.ts`** (pure, tested): fable → black (about 3.0 s of true silence, the room's
  ambience held at 0) → `beginOpening()` (`src/App.ts:1273`).
- **`App.ts`:** `enter()` plays the fable when `!readFableSeen()`; `hh.fable-seen` is written at
  the end or on Skip and cleared by `forgetVisits`. The Director stays held, the rig disabled and
  the picture black throughout. On fable visits the threshold hides `#title` visually so its first
  appearance comes after the film.
- **Watching again:** "the fable" beside "go in again" on the threshold, then the returning score;
  and "watch the fable" in the settings menu, which hushes and holds the room, plays the film, the
  black and the title, then lets the room return.
- **Tests:** `?nofable` for the existing automated runs; `tests/fable.test.ts` (stages, the seen
  key, captions covering every narration line exactly once and in order); `tests/playtest/fable.mjs`
  on phone and desktop (play with audio, captions, pause, Skip → black → title → room, the end
  transition through a debug seek, watching again, and a broken film URL reaching the title in
  under 10 s).

### Questions for the reviewers

1. **Length.** A film of 3:15–4:00 before a first visitor reaches the room. Is that right for this
   work, and what in the film earns it (or should be shorter: the separate nights, the curtains)?
2. **Cutting from a 500 px reference.** The fox in the reference is roughly 250–450 px across.
   Upscaled and re-posed as cards, will he hold up in close shots (panel 4's close-up, the reach),
   and what should the framing avoid?
3. **2.5D cards in EEVEE versus the alternatives:** a 2D compositing pipeline in Python (no 3D),
   or the cards drawn live in Three.js in the browser (no video, frame-exact with audio, but mobile
   GPU cost and weaker shadows and glow).
4. **Phones.** Two framings (16:9 and 4:5) doubles render time. Is a 4:5 cut needed, or is a
   16:9 film letterboxed on an upright phone acceptable for an opening?
5. **Reduced motion** for a film: a locked-camera variant (another render), or simply no camera
   pushes anywhere in the film so one version serves everyone?
6. **Music** at all, given the room's "no music in the opening" and the bible's sparse cues.
7. **Voice.** What to listen for in the audition, and the risk of a library voice that sounds like
   a bedtime-story or audiobook narrator.
8. **Where the room's first sound comes from** after the silent title, and whether the existing
   opening score (`src/state/OpeningScore.ts`) still fits after a narrated film.

## 3. Approach review (2026-10-31)

Five specialists reviewed section 2 in parallel (creative, cinematic, sound, technical art,
interaction), and the skeptic challenged their reports. Their full reports were kept in the session;
what they found is summarised here, and section 4 records what was decided and why.

**Measured on this machine** (technical artist, confirmed by the skeptic):
- **EEVEE's ray tracing does not work on the UHD 630.** A mirror plane with a planar probe shows
  only the world, under OpenGL and Vulkan alike; screen-traced reflections of on-screen objects
  fail too. Cycles on the CPU reflects the same scene. The probe still costs 18–25 s a frame.
- **Solidify and bevel show nothing along an alpha cut.** A flat card with a normal map baked from
  the matte's distance field gives a lit edge at any angle.
- **A realistic pond frame takes 40–48 s** without the probe (151 objects, about 130 alpha cards,
  1080p, 32 samples), 81 s at 1920×1440 with it. 32 samples are enough. Region renders aren't
  seamless in EEVEE, so Almost's region-paste method doesn't carry over. A whole film is about
  13–39 h per final; a half-size EEVEE draft about 5 h; Workbench 0.3–0.7 s a frame but without
  the scene's lights.
- **A layered numpy/OpenCV compositor** of the same pond (30 set layers, 16 warped fox pieces lit
  from baked normals, contact shadows, a mirrored reflection, haze, a light gain) runs at about
  1.5 s a frame at 1920×1440: about 2 h for the film (skeptic). A Cycles plate of the static set,
  which does reflect, takes about 3 minutes.
- **The anime 6B upscaler turns soft painting into cel shading** (high-frequency energy 6.19
  against 3.56 for Lanczos on the woodland sheet). The fox in the reference is 243–270 px across in
  panels 2, 4 and 5 and about 95 px in 1 and 6; his eye is about 6×4 px. He holds about 3× as
  raster; his colour is already consistent across panels (hue 20°, S 0.89–0.92, V 0.41–0.47).
- **Standard keeps him orange in the dark; AgX looks don't** (at 25% exposure Standard gives hue
  19°, AgX Punchy a dark red). Light linking works in EEVEE 5.2.
- **Contact shadows need gaps of about 4 mm** between paper layers, and EEVEE's soft penumbrae
  speckle (they would boil on twos).
- **File sizes** from a 12 s proxy: a 3:30 phone film at 864×1080 is about 6 MB (VP9 + Opus) or
  13 MB (H.264 + AAC); desktop 1080p about 10 MB (VP9) or 23 MB (H.264). The room's films are
  fetched whole as blobs, which would take about 20 s for the iPhone file on a 5 Mb/s connection.
- **On an upright phone** a letterboxed 16:9 picture is 390×219 CSS px: the fox on the hill is about
  50 px across and his head about 12 px, so the final head turn doesn't exist. A 4:5 picture is
  390×488 with the fox about 120 px.

**Found in the code** (interaction and sound, verified where marked):
- As proposed, the fable would play after `enter()` has already set `mode = 'room'`
  (`src/App.ts:356`) and before `this.opening` exists, so none of the opening's guards apply: Esc
  opens settings, T opens the writing field, K kneels, the back gesture opens a "paused" settings
  dialog over a film that keeps playing, a hidden tab suspends the AudioContext but not the video,
  and the 40 s watchdog doesn't cover it.
- The room's ambience starts at the tap (hum, vent, outside, drone at 1), so it would play under the
  film; `silence()` bottoms out at −30 dB; and the outside layer is still faintly audible under the
  title (verified).
- The returning score has no title (`src/state/OpeningScore.ts:107`) and a switch 0.6 s in, so a
  re-watch from the threshold would end film → black → click → room (verified).
- Routing the film through `createMediaElementSource` loses native A/V sync and, on iPhones without
  `navigator.audioSession`, puts the narration under the ringer switch.
- A film at −16 LUFS would be about 10 LU louder than the room's first voice (arithmetic through the
  chain; to be measured).
- Chromium remembers the tap ("sticky activation"), so a passing autoplay test there proves
  nothing about iOS.

**The main disagreements** and the skeptic's view:
- **Music:** the creative director wants none under the narrator (at most one short figure in the
  wordless ease); the sound designer wanted a call-and-answer phrase with an unfinished reprise. The
  skeptic sided with the creative director on what, and with the sound designer on how to decide it
  (an A/B the artist hears).
- **The opening image:** the creative director wanted to open on him telling, not gazing; the
  skeptic pointed out the narration's own order (he sits, then tells) and that panels 1 and 6 are
  the artist's arc in two frames.
- **"Too much light":** the creative and cinematic directors wanted no beam at all; the skeptic
  showed panel 3's shaft is broad and soft, floods the bank, and is the moon's only way to reach.
- **The closest shot:** the cinematic director put it on "trying not to cry" because ripples forgive
  the upscaled pieces; the creative director and the skeptic called that a render constraint
  choosing the emotional peak.
- **Narration:** both directors' recording plans relied on seeds and `previous_text`, which the
  connector's v4 doesn't offer.
- **The render route:** see decision 1.

## 4. Decisions after the skeptic (2026-10-31)

1. **The render route is the artist's to choose, on a bake-off.** *(Decided 2026-11-01: B, the
   compositor.)*
   - EEVEE was chosen assuming a GPU on which its reflections work. They don't here, and once the
     water, contact shadows, glows, haze and edges are all built as explicit cards (decision 2),
     per-frame EEVEE contributes only direct light on cards, at 13–39 h a final and with drafts
     (Workbench) that can't show the moon's light.
   - Proposed: before cutting the pieces, a bake-off on shots 4–6 (withdraw, brighten, dim, rest).
     **A:** EEVEE cards. **B:** a layered compositor in Python (RGBA cards with baked edge normals lit
     per light, shadow cards, a flipped reflection under a water card with the strip break, gauze
     haze, the light script as per-light gains), optionally over Cycles set plates rendered once
     per light per shot in Blender. Stills beside panels 2–3 and the motion on the artist's phone.
   - Recommendation: B unless A looks clearly better. B keeps Blender for staging the set and its
     light, and gives a whole-film draft in hours instead of days.
   - **Bake-off run (2026-10-31).**
     - Setup: the pond beat (35 s, withdraw → brighter → silence → dim → rest → creep) was cut
       from panel 2 by `scripts/fable/cut.py` (24 layers). It was timed once in
       `scripts/fable/timeline.py` and rendered by `stage.py` (A, EEVEE cards) and `comp.py` (B,
       linear-light compositor) at 1152×1440, 420 drawings on twos. `bakeoff.py` makes the phone
       clips, a side-by-side and a stills sheet beside panels 2–3. The output is in
       `test-output/fable/bake/review/`.
     - Picture: indistinguishable at phone size. The one difference is that when her reflection
       softens, A's goes smooth and B's keeps the paper grain, because B mixes the alphas but keeps
       the sharp layer's colour (a fix in B, not a limit of the route).
     - Cost per drawing: A 7.9 s (the first takes 29 s while EEVEE compiles shaders). B 4.4 s, and
       4.3 s when rendered again alone (so sharing the CPU for part of its run barely mattered). The whole film at the
       3:30 ceiling (2,520 drawings) is about 6 h for A and 3 h for B at this size, more at the full
       open matte. Both fit in one night, so cost doesn't decide it.
     - Faults shared by both routes, in the cut and not the route:
       - his reflection reads as a reddish smear;
       - the creep is four hard pale strips;
       - there's a seam where the patched bank meets the original reeds on the right;
       - the mud barely shows.

       They get fixed in `cut.py` before the fox is cut for the other shots.
     - Still recommended: B. The same picture in about half the time; any frame in seconds without
       Blender; plain Python the tests can check. A's real depth only pays if the camera moves
       (decision 5 locks it). **The artist decides.**
   - **The artist chose B (2026-11-01)** after watching both on the phone.
     - `comp.py` renders the film.
     - The paper theatre stays 2.5D: depths, her direction and the light script live in
       `timeline.py`, and the shadows fall by depth gap.
     - Blender leaves the render path. `stage.py` stays as the bake-off's record and as a way to
       check a shot in real depth if one ever needs it.
     - B now draws her soft reflection with its own blurred colour, as A did.
   - **The shared faults, fixed in the cut (2026-11-01).** Checked at phone size against the bake-off's
     B (`bakeoff.py --pair B_clip B_fix`).
     - His reflection is the fox, mud and all, lit as he is but at 30%, a little cooler. It is
       broken into the water's strips as the panel breaks hers: each strip slides sideways, more
       nearer the lens, so the edges go ragged. The water's own glints stay over him, and he comes
       up to 0.85. It reads as him upside down in the water, beside her.
     - The creep is six thin glints of her light, tapered and broken like the panel's far-water
       glints. They run from her reflection's edge to the water under his bank. Each one slides its
       last few px toward him and then shimmers. They are drawn as added light, not pale paint.
     - The seam:
       - The bank ends where the panel's does. Its top is broken earth with a lit shelf under his
         nose.
       - The rock under him is carried in from the bare stretch in front of him, not blurred.
       - The plate keeps the right reeds, which never move and have their own layer over them; this
         removes the ghost reeds.
       - Behind his head, the sky's glints are carried in random tiles and the far shore row by
         row, so nothing repeats.
     - The mud stays one state (decision 7). It is now splashes on four pieces: the tail, the flank
       and white bib, the foreleg, the chin and cheek. Each is dark at the heart and paler at the
       edge, and big enough to read on a phone. They show in his reflection too.
     - Also found and fixed: the pieces' grown edges carried sky with him (a pale halo), and the
       head and foreleg outlines overshot, so a sliver of jaw and sky rode along when he moved. The
       outlines are retraced. Just inside each cut, anything that isn't fur is keyed out; just
       outside it, only his orange rim is taken in.
2. **Whatever the route, build the effects as cards.** (Technical artist, accepted.)
   - The reflection is a mirrored copy of the set and fox under a semi-transparent water card. Her
     reflected disc is a stack of horizontal strips. The sky moon is never part of the reflection.
   - Contact shadows are a shadow card per piece (blurred alpha, offset along the key). Glows are
     additive (emission over transparent), so their order doesn't matter.
   - No planar probe, no solidify and bevel on alpha cards, no screen-space grain ever.
   - Standard view transform, Look None. His lit side stays at 0.8–1.1× his texture value in every
     shot, with a warm fill linked to him alone; the set and the sky carry the darkness. Nothing
     emissive above 1.0.
3. **Cutting him.** (Technical artist, with the skeptic's caveat.)
   - Edges from hand-drawn vector paths (like `FIG_POLY` in `scripts/cut-woodland.py`), the colour
     layer resampled 3–4× and de-lit, and one shared paper grain at one physical scale. Eye, nose,
     mouth line and split pins rebuilt as small separate pieces; one master pin layout for every
     panel's pieces, with a round overlap at each pin.
   - The anime 6B upscale is not used by default, but the artist chose it for the woodland, so they
     see one fox piece both ways at phone size and decide.
   - The painted rim: first check at 864×1080 whether a re-posed rim lit from the wrong side is even
     visible; build the baked-normal edge only if it is.
   - Framing limits: the whole fox at most half the frame's width, his head at most a third of its
     height.
4. **Framing: one open matte, composed in a 4:5 column.** (Cinematic, technical and interaction,
   with the skeptic's simplification.)
   - Every shot is rendered at 1920×1440 and composed so what must be seen together lies in the
     central 4:5 column. Phones get the 4:5 picture at 864×1080 (never a half-size copy).
   - The desktop film is either a 16:9 crop or the same 4:5 picture pillarboxed in the theatre's
     dark; the artist decides at lookdev. No second framing is rendered.
5. **The camera is locked.** (Cinematic, accepted; the skeptic didn't contest it.) No pushes, no
   parallax. Depth comes from pieces crossing layers and light through layers; light dips within a
   place, hard cuts between places, no dissolves, a hard cut to black. One version serves reduced
   motion.
6. **The shot list is the cinematic director's sixteen shots**, with these changes:
   - **Shot 1:** panel 1's pose (gazing up) for ¶1 only, about 4 s; telling, with a head tilt and an
     ear flick, from ¶2. (The skeptic's resolution: the narration's own order, and the artist's
     image.) The pool of light on the empty crest carries "the moon waits for him".
   - **Shot 5** is at the two-shot's distance, not close: his reflection, dark until now, comes up
     small beside hers. No crying close-up. (Creative director and skeptic over the cinematic
     director.) If the film has a closer frame anywhere, it is the ease.
   - The ease (shot 2) is play rather than repose, maybe rolling onto his back. The longest wordless
     hold.
   - No walk cycles: he rises over the hill's ground row or slides in from a wing. Lists in the
     narration (blackberries, a bird) are not illustrated.
7. **Her light is a written performance** (cinematic director 3), with one change.
   - Her key comes from where she is. The pool on the crest has three states: waiting, absent,
     already there. "Disappearing" softens and dims her reflection; the dimming he asks for later is
     the same look, deeper. The creep is paper strips of light laid one at a time toward his bank.
     Amber is only his rim. At the end her halo leans toward the pond and the far glint rises about
     20%; the pool stays.
   - **"Too much light"** is a broad, soft shaft from where she is, across the pond and the bank,
     never following him (panel 3; skeptic over the creative and cinematic directors). What exposes
     him is his own reflection coming up beside hers (creative director). If a phone test shows the
     general lift alone reads, the shaft goes.
   - **"Muddy coat":** one muddy state, shown when her light rises. (Skeptic over the creative
