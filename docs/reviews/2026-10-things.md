# Things I Should Have Said (memory III): review record

A charcoal and pencil memory on one sheet: an ordinary goodbye at an apartment's front door, a
young man leaving and his mother staying, then the drawing redrawing the moment with what might have
been done, erasing each, until only an overworked doorway is left. The artist's brief is "THE
HEDGEHOG'S DILEMMA / MEMORY III — THINGS I SHOULD HAVE SAID" (2026-10-21). The decisions below
follow it; the reviews and their outcomes are added as they happen.

## 1. Research and the architecture decision (2026-10-21)

**What the brief asks of the medium.** Real charcoal and graphite behaviour, in the manner of
Kentridge's Drawings for Projection: one sheet worked over, a mark laid, partly erased, the residue
left, a new mark over it. Not a sketch filter. Proved first on stills (Proof A: the figures in the
doorway, and a second pose beginning a gesture) and a short sequence (Proof B: deposit, erase,
residue, redraw), before any animatic.

**The machine.** 4 CPU cores, no GPU, Blender 5.2.2 headless (Cycles on the CPU; no EGL, so no
EEVEE or Workbench), Python with NumPy and OpenCV. No paid services.

| Option | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| A. A simulated sheet (NumPy/OpenCV): pigment and paper as state, marks as events | **Chosen for the medium** |
| B. Stylise rendered frames (a sketch filter, NPR shaders, Line Art drawn as is) | Rejected: the brief names it as the failure; nothing accumulates, nothing is erased |
| C. Neural image or video models | Rejected: GPU-bound, not deterministic, and the brief asks for a hand's logic, not a texture |
| D. Hand-made drawings by the artist, scanned | Kept open: the creative director's first choice for the figures (section 3); the engine can take scanned drawings as deposited layers |
| E. A 3D reference for what to draw: Blender, Grease Pencil Line Art, a tonal render, object IDs | **Chosen for the reference** |

**The sheet (`scripts/things/paper.py`).** Loose and held charcoal, loose and held graphite, and the
paper's wear, over a grain (the tooth). Each tool deposits through the tooth (a light mark catches
only the peaks); the eraser lifts more from the peaks than the valleys, little of what is ground
in, drags crumbs at the end of its stroke and roughs the paper; a finger or rag spreads loose
pigment. A mark's footprint is made once, every pixel tagged with how far along the mark it lies, so
a mark made over twelve drawings is exactly the mark made at once. Every irregularity is seeded by
the mark's name.

**The reference (`scripts/things/scene.py`).** The doorway, the corridor beyond it and two people,
seen by a camera that never moves. Per pose: Line Art contours per thing (hidden lines removed), a
tonal render, an object-ID render, the long bones and a few points of each face on the paper.

**The drawing (`scripts/things/draw.py`).** What an artist does with such a reference, in order:
light construction, the masses with the stick's side in passes toward the tone, a little rubbing,
the lights lifted, then the lines, lost and found. **The film (`timeline.py`, `film.py`).** Marks
with a start and a length, drawings on twos streamed into the encoder; each revision planned on the
film's own sheet at the moment it starts. **The sound (`foley.py`).** Made from the same list of
marks.

## 2. Proof A, first version: reviews (2026-10-21)

The first figures were skin-modifier bodies on a hand-made armature. The creative director and
the skeptic: **mannequins** (paddle hands, stump feet, no shoulder girdle, glyph faces, clothes as
body shells), the gestures unreadable (a raised arm hidden behind his back; a wave that read as a
phone call), the tone a uniform speckle, the lines traced Line Art, the doorway outside the wide
screen's band. Their split verdict: **keep the paper engine, replace the figure source**.

### Decisions

1. **The figures are MakeHuman's (CC0), built in Blender by the MPFB add-on** (2.0.18, installed
   from its release; the system asset pack unpacked into its data folder): real anatomy, hands,
   clothes and hair, on MakeHuman's default skeleton, posed by rotations about the figure's own
   axes and a four-bone arm IK. Building both takes under 4 s.
2. **The gestures are small and do not match** (the creative director): she waves, small, at her
   shoulder; he is already going, the bag she packed lifted instead of a hand, his eyes on the
   corridor. The almosts: he comes back to rest a hand on the jamb, his weight back, his head and
   shoulder dropping toward her; she takes half a step and her hand rises toward his collar.
3. **The camera stands 6 m back** (28 mm, level, 1.1 m up), so the lintel and the threshold are
   inside the band a wide screen shows: the last image is a doorway on every screen.
4. **The corridor is lit** and the room has an even fill, so he is dark against light and the room's
   paper stays mostly bare: the room drawn at a third of its tonal weight, the people at all of it.
5. **Lost and found contours**: where the tone shows an edge, the line comes and goes; where it
   cannot (a lit figure on a lit wall), the line carries the form at a weight that holds it.
6. **No drips**: walls and casing hatched on the diagonal; the door and the floor rubbed across.

## 4. Proof A3 and Proof B3: the skeptic (2026-10-21)

The skeptic: the paper engine works, and the ghost of him walking under a solid him at the jamb is
the best image the memory has produced; but the goodbye reads as a hand to the mouth, the imagined
son ends more solid than the real one, the collar's near-miss exists only in 3D (88-111 px apart
in the picture, the long lens flattening the depth), the doorway the film ends on is never worked,
the film shows its build order at 60-90 strokes a second, both heads read as bald, the room's
diagonal masses read as light through blinds, and Foley on every mark makes the engine audible.

### Decisions after the skeptic

7. **Only what happened has weight.** The real goodbye is drawn in charcoal, in tone. What is
   imagined is drawn in line only (no masses, no rubbing, no lifts), and the line is erased. The
   imagined can never become more solid than what happened.
8. **A revision is slow and partial.** Only the region that changes is redrawn, its lines over
   3-4 s from the moving hand, so a hand can be seen drawing; the figure's construction lines are
   gone (they read as a life-drawing diagram).
9. **Every almost works the doorway.** Each revision's erasure is allowed over the casing nearest
   the moving hand, and the casing is restated there in charcoal, displaced a little: by the end
   the doorway is the most worked place on the sheet, from both sides.
10. **Erasure is uneven and incomplete**: some scrubs skipped, some parts left (a shoe, the bag);
    stopped when it is enough, not when the mask is covered.
11. **The goodbye is ordinary**: her wave is a lift of the fingers at her ribs, clear of her face;
    the light switch is gone (it read as a phone or a glass in her hand).
12. **Hair is a mass** (its own region: his dark, hers a mid grey, no lifts on it); **he has no
    face** (in his memory he does not see his own); hers keeps the three accents.
13. **The room is line**, its tone only a rubbed contact shadow under each person.
14. **About 50 s, no montage of fragments**: the goodbye drawn and played (the wave, his walk out,
    the door swinging shut on its closer, the latch), his almost, her almost, the people erased,
    the empty doorway held.
15. **Her almost stays a reach toward him** (the brief: "the older one almost reaches"), judged in
    the picture: her hand ends a hand's width from his collar on the paper, not in metres. The
    skeptic's alternative, both reaching only for the doorway's two sides, and the repetition with
    Memory II's reach are put to the artist.
16. **The sound keeps the brief's voices but leads with the eraser**: drawing is quiet, taking back
    is heard; the latch once, when he has gone. An eraser-only mix is made beside it for the
    artist's A/B (the skeptic: "adding is silent; taking back is heard").
17. `features.py` (dead) is deleted.

## 5. The animatic: the creative and cinematic directors (2026-10-21)

Both: the structure is right and the first 40 s work (the best images: the eraser lifting the shut
door and the ghosts of his walk coming up through it, 17-18.4 s; her line self stepping out of her
charcoal body, 35-37 s). The ending fails: the "empty doorway" held three line people, the imagined
son the darkest (99th-percentile darkness 92-95 below the paper, against 63-64 for her real figure),
the last 8 s static. Measured besides: the casing never restated (`casing_marks` returned nothing:
sparse vertices fell outside its circle); the latch 1.65 s after the door was home and no louder
than its drawing; the walk lightening a step at a time (tone strokes 107, 52, 1); the take-backs
invisible though they were the loudest sounds; the two almosts on equal cycles; the band too tight
(her toes on its edge); the opening drawing the real people as line first. A full-resolution film
takes 2 min 9 s to make (49 s planning), 833 MB at its peak: checkpoints are not worth keeping.

### Decisions after the directors

18. **What is imagined is graphite**, never charcoal: lighter than anything that happened, cleaner
    to erase; its outline only (its inner lines dropped: his lapel read as a black tie); its hand
    drawn first and heaviest.
19. **The ending**: everyone erased, the heads entirely (a ghost is a person while it has a head);
    what touched the doorway left (his hand's mark on the right jamb, her fingers' at the left
    casing); then both casings drawn again, his side and then hers, so the doorway is the last
    thing a hand touches; then the empty doorway. The film is 52 s.
20. **Her almost is her arm alone**, from where she stands: its pinched fingers stop at the left
    casing, at his collar's height, the doorway between her hand and him; taken back before its
    lines are done (the hand changes its mind). No second head, no second body.
21. **His almost holds longest** (3 s) and is taken back visibly, from the hand (the arm's region
    wider, the eraser full strength).
22. **The walk keeps his weight**: each step takes him out entire and draws him again at full tone,
    0.8 s apart; the last two half behind the casing, then only his shoulder and the bag.
23. **The door slows on its closer** (-82, -55, -30, -12, -4, home at 14.5 s) and the latch is on
    the drawing that brings it home, set after the level so it is the clearest sound of what
    happened; then 4 s of silence before the first almost.
24. **The opening draws tone before line**: the doorway's construction, the people in passes a hand
    makes, their lines found last. A real person is never line alone on the sheet.
25. **Her wave is seen**: two rocks of the wrist, larger, before his walk takes the eye.
26. **The camera stands 6.5 m back, 1 m up**: the doorway and both of them inside the band with room.
    The table is gone (in line it read as a sawhorse); her hair is a darker mass.
27. **The sound is eraser-only by default** (drawing silent, erasing heard, the latch), with the
    eraser-led mix beside it for the artist; the `full` mix is dropped.
28. **Her ending is the artist's call**: erased with everyone (the brief's empty doorway, and the
    default), or her real figure rubbed back by half and left (the creative director's preference:
    she stayed, he is the one who is gone; erasing her points toward "she died"). Both are made.

## 6. The sound: the sound designer (2026-10-21)

The Foley was mixed by tool, not by meaning: his real walk-out (each step scrubbed out and drawn
again) sounded as erasing, the imagined son made the loudest peak of the film, the latch was 1.65 s
late, 17 LU under the eraser and mostly a 92 Hz sine a phone cannot play, the eraser a flat
plateau with no hand in it, `side_firm` unmapped, and each mix normalised so the A/B moved the
eraser too.

### Decisions

29. **Each mark is heard by what it means**: taking back (the eraser on the door, the almosts, the
    end) is the reference; what happened 14 LU under it; what is imagined 24 LU under, or silent.
    The step scrubs of his walk are a hand's swish, not erasing. Mixes: `eraser-only` (the
    default), `eraser`, `weight` (the imagined drawn in silence); the artist chooses.
30. **One hand**: one drawing tool sounds at a time (the loudest, crossfaded); where it is moves as
    an arm does (300 ms); speed is a hand's at most.
31. **The eraser is a hand scrubbing**: 3-6 strokes a second, a dip at each turn, push and pull
    unequal, stick-slip chatter and the paper's rustle, a knock at each reversal; it ends easing
    off, and the hand lifts before the silence.
32. **The latch is on the drawing that brings the door home**, rebuilt (two clicks over the strike
    and into the keeper, metal modes, the slab's knock in 150-600 Hz, a rattle), its loudest moment
    5 LU under the eraser's, set after the level.
33. **Levels are absolute**: the eraser at -33.5 LUFS while it sounds (Memory II's figures), the same
    in every mix; drawing peaks never above its peaks. The film's small room and air are for the
    film alone; the room's copy leaves them out.

## 7. The finished film in the room: the creative and cinematic directors, QA (2026-10-21)

The creative director's final evaluation found the knob and a hook left in the empty doorway (read
as a locked door), her feet's found line read as shoes, and a headless outline of her as the one
image left. The cinematic director (on a022e57) found that her almost's take-back erased her real
head and torso (the tone term of `film.delta_mask` pulled her lit body into the imagined region),
so "hers taken back" and "everyone erased" were one beat and the two endings looked alike. Her
almost was also drawn whole and held, and the final restatement drew a near-black hook in the door
(`draw.casing_marks` took any arch stroke near the hand: the corridor's rail). The trace after the
exit was below what a screen shows within a second, and the empty doorway was held about 5 s. The
take-backs panned about 1 dB. QA found the crop constant at every aspect (the lintel cut and her
feet on the edge on a landscape phone and at 21:9), `THINGS_BEATS` hand-copied with nothing holding
it to the sheet (the casings were 2.4 s later than the announcements said), the film's sound
playing on over the still when the film failed, only one sound captioned, the H.264 at Level 5.0,
and some faint line pixels with no time in the order map, popping in at 7.5 s.

### Decisions

34. **She stays by default**: her real figure rubbed back evenly by half, her head kept (the
    creative director's ruling on the finished film: the doorway is empty, the flat is not).
    `story.py --erase-her 1` makes the brief's empty doorway for the artist. Supersedes 28's default.
35. **Her almost is her arm alone**: drawn and erased inside the reaching arm's mask less her real
    figure, never by the tone that changed on her body.
36. **Her almost is caught while it is drawn**: hand first, only the lines nearest the hand ever
    made, the eraser in 1.4 s after it begins, so the upper arm is never drawn (his is drawn whole
    and held; hers changes its mind).
37. **The last touch is the casing's**: every restatement works casing pixels only; the knob goes
    with everyone; her feet have no found line; her reach is level, the hand open.
38. **The doorway is held longer than the latch's silence**: the film is 50 s in the room (56 s on
    the sheet), the casings done at 53.6 s, the exit at 60.25 s; the trace stays readable under the
    returning words (0.4, then 0.28) before it goes into the plaster.
39. **The take-backs are heard where the hand is** (pan to about ±6 dB at the frame's edges).
40. **The crop follows the screen**: the drawing's middle while the lintel and her feet fit with room;
    wider, the room above the lintel goes first, then (from about 1.95:1) the lintel; her feet
    never (`tests/things-cover.test.ts`).
41. **The beats are read off the sheet** (`emergence.py` writes them beside the sound's provenance;
    `tests/film-scene.test.ts` holds `THINGS_BEATS` to them within a frame). The latch is captioned;
    a film that cannot be had takes its sound with it; the H.264 is Level 4.0 (4 references); every
    line pixel the wall draws has a time.

Deferred: the phrase carried faintly beneath the film (the creative director: only the wash cap,
0.35, is done); the soft edge made from the eraser's own scrubs rather than a cloud (both directors:
the technical artist's); the film loading in the background before it is paused (QA, shared with
Almost); output latency at the latch on a real phone. Not changed, for the artist and the
interaction designer: as a reserved sixth memory, Things does not come in a first visit, and its
line is no longer written in one.

## 8. v2: the artist's review of the first film (2026-10-22)

The artist: the film read as "a departure followed by the gradual alteration and erasure of a
drawing"; it must be "a mind desperately but quietly trying to revise a goodbye that has already
happened". The revisions were hard to tell from the departure; the figures were small, the older one
static; erasure looked like dissolution; the middle had long stretches where nothing developed; the
ending kept a standing figure. They asked for three readable events (the goodbye; his turning back,
unfinished; her reaching, unfinished), physical and directional erasure, construction before a
revised gesture, both people gone at the end with only traces of what was seen, about 30-45 s if
earned, and sound synchronised to the marks with silence used deliberately. The first film is kept
as the baseline (commit 5d27d7b; its renders in `test-output/review/things/baseline-v1/`).

Reviewed on rendered animatics, in turn: the creative director and the cinematic director (as
animation director) on the second pass, the skeptic on the third. All three agreed the structure now
works and that his turning back is the best moment the piece has had. Their main findings: the
walk's residue stood in the doorway as a crowd of him to the last frame, so the ending read as a
ghost image (all three; the skeptic found the end erasure missed his last positions); her imagined
reach repeated a reach she really made at the goodbye (creative); the turn away was a cut, his head
turn did not read, his stages ticked like a metronome and the holds were thin (cinematic, skeptic);
the revising pencil was inaudible (cinematic) and the end erasure as loud as the take-backs
(skeptic); the final casing restatement was the darkest, meaningless mark (creative); her pre-rub
read as her starting to vanish (skeptic); his hand's impression sat on the doorknob (skeptic).

### Decisions

42. **The camera comes in a little and the two of them are staged in depth**: she in the room near
    us, left; he between her and the door, facing her. He turns his back on her and walks away from
    her, out. (On a 16:9 screen the doorway already filled the band's height: what grows is her, and
    the silhouettes; on an upright phone the view is 1.22 times closer than covering, the drawing
    whole across.)
43. **The goodbye is fact**: her hands low, a small wave at her waist; he lifts the bag and nods;
    a three-quarter turn; three positions out; each taken out entire, head and lines, so nothing of
    the walk is left (supersedes the Kentridge step's residue for what happened: the residue made the
    real event look already revised, and filled the last image).
44. **What is imagined is found, drawn in stages and taken back** (film.py `imagine`, `revise`,
    `withdraw`): construction in hard pencil first (the artist's request; the skeptic would cut it);
    each stage rubs the lines it changes and draws over their ghost; the taking back runs along the
    drawn lines, back and forth, with one band across the arm, the hand least.
45. **His**: in the doorway, his back as he left, held; he turns back toward her (shoulder and head
    in one stage, his face drawn for the first time); a hesitation; his free hand rises, starts,
    stops, goes on; held 1.9 s; taken back. **Hers**, after a 2 s breath: from where she stands, a
    lean after him in line over her toned body, her hand rising toward the doorway; held 1.1 s; taken
    back. Their hands nearly meet across the left casing.
46. **The ending**: a breath; she is taken (head first), then where he stood; both quieter than the
    almosts; the two hands' impressions spared. No casing restated (the creative director over the
    cinematic director, who wanted it heard as the last touch: it was the last image's darkest mark
    and referred to nothing seen). Six seconds of the empty doorway before the room lets it go.
47. **The sound**: the default mix `revisit` leaves what happened silent but the latch; the revising
    pencil is heard faintly (-13 LU), its rubs under the eraser (-7), the taking back the reference,
    the ending's erasure well under it (-11).
48. **The pacing**: 42.75 s in the room (48.75 on the sheet), the latch's silence 3.6 s, the door on
    three drawings. The skeptic's further cuts (construction lines, the band) are declined: the
    first is the artist's own request, the second is cheap and the creative director's.

Not done, for the artist: a cold viewer (no outside viewer has seen it); the phone check on a real
device; whether her hand's impression should be as present as his at the end.

### The finished film (creative and cinematic directors, 2026-10-22)

Both directors judged the finished v2 to work as a piece, with nothing at P0: the goodbye is fact,
the two revisions read and shorten (his 12 s, hers 6, the ending under 3), the silence after the
latch holds, the empty doorway lands. The cinematic director withdrew the objection to cutting the
casing restatement ("the finished film shows the creative director was right"). Their remaining
P1s: her imagined hand touched his drawn one, which resolves what the piece keeps open (creative);
the take-backs of the outlines still read as an even lightening (creative); the revising pencil was
still inaudible, 32 LU under the eraser (cinematic); his walk stayed a cloud behind the light door
under the silence, and two doorknob ghosts, not the hands, were the last image's darkest marks
(both).

49. **Her reach stops a finger's width short of his hand**, and that gap is held.
50. **Every taking back crosses the body**: a few strokes at an angle to the back, stopping short,
    with a grey drag, besides the band across the arm and the strokes along the lines.
51. **The revising pencil is set by its measured loudness**, 14 LU under the eraser's (as a gain on a
    voice already far under, it was inaudible).
52. **Where he walked is cleared before the door shuts on it; the door's old knob goes as it moves;
    at the end the knobs and what is left of the almosts are thinned, except the two hands**, which
    are the last image's darkest marks.

## 9. The soundscape: Mix A and Mix B (2026-10-22)

The artist's sound brief: an ordinary goodbye in an inhabited, extraordinarily quiet flat, then an
almost imperceptible withdrawal into recollection; close charcoal, graphite and eraser Foley as the
identity, synced to the drawing, never continuous scratching; the first revision intimate, the
second heavier, the accumulation uncomfortable, not horror; the ending's sounds disappearing, the
last doorway near-silent: "the silence should make us feel that the thought hasn't stopped". Two
mixes, A (sound design) and B (a restrained, Ushio-like underscore, "if the film is more powerful
without music, do not force it").

`scripts/things/soundscape.py` makes both from the exposure sheet: all procedural, the piano alone
recorded (FreePats Upright Piano KW, CC0). Nobody has listened: the reviews below judged levels,
spectra, onsets against the marks and the synthesis itself.

### The first mixes (sound designer, cinematic director; the skeptic)

Both chose A. Measured faults common to both mixes:
- **His taking back was louder than hers** (his peak −21.9, hers −24.9): the door's lift shared his
  stem and its quiet median pulled the stem up.
- **The ending rose** to the knobs and the almosts, the thinnest act given the ending's loudest sound.
- **A pencil set down** after the last mark was the film's one full stop.
- **The eraser fell silent ten frames before the paper stopped changing.**
- **The pencil was one hiss with a buzz of ticks** (36–80 a second).
- **The wall's drawing** was the loudest thing before his taking back.
- **The redraw's rubs** inside his turn were as loud as the lift, heard as a taking back before
  anything was taken (the skeptic).
- **B's held tone** filled the holds and breaths by 7–12 LU, pumped under every erasure, and faded
  like an ending; its 1.1 Hz beat a tremolo that may read as a test tone.

The skeptic's correction to both: the room plays the film 9 dB down over its own hushed hum. Below
about −55 LUFS in the file nothing reaches a visitor, so the argument over the doorway's faint
presence was over an inaudible sound, and so were the faint flat, steps and pencil.

### Decisions

54. **Each taking back is levelled on its own** (the lift −37.5 median, his −32, hers −28), and
    hers is heavier in kind, too: slower strokes, harder knocks at the turns, the paper's give at
    250–700 Hz where a phone keeps it.
55. **The redraw's rubs are 14 LU under the reference**, under the pencil they make room for.
56. **The ending fades as it goes**, each erasure quieter (10 dB from the first to the last: at 15,
    where he stood went under the room's hush as it was seen going: the sound designer's evaluation).
57. **Nothing after the last mark**: no pencil set down, no paper's presence; the room's hushed air is
    the last silence. (Not an unanswered pencil tick, the cinematic director's alternative: a sound
