# Almost (memory II): review record

A felt and wool stop-motion film, about 30 s, set in a small kitchen at night. Two handmade
figures sit side by side at a table under a hanging lamp. One straightens the other's folded
sleeve, the other startles, the hand goes back to the lap, the other moves a little closer, and it
is too late. The artist's brief is "THE HEDGEHOG'S DILEMMA, MEMORY II — ALMOST" (2026-10-20). The
decisions below follow it; the reviews and their outcomes are added as they happen.

## 1. Audit (2026-10-20)

**The machine.**
- 4 CPU cores, 15 GB RAM, no GPU.
- About 2 GB of disk is free for the session.
- Blender 5.2.2 LTS is installed at `/opt/blender` and runs headless.
  - **Cycles on the CPU works**, with OpenImageDenoise.
  - **EEVEE and Workbench do not:** there is no EGL, so they crash in background mode.
  - Particle hair, armatures, IK constraints and shape keys all work from `bpy`.
  - A first material test rendered at 640×360 and 64 samples in 20 s.

**The app** (summarised from a full read of `src/memory`, `src/world`, `App.ts` and the tests).
- **What a memory is.** A memory is a `SceneMemory`, which says when and from which line, plus a
  `MemoryDef`, which says what it is. It also needs an art manifest and a saved Theatre sheet.
- **One clock.** There is one clock, the memory's audio. Everything visible is a pure function of
  the timeline position, exact when held or seeked. No shader may compile after boot.
- **The living path.** Only the woodland afternoon is a "living" memory. It is drawn into a render
  target that becomes `MemoryWindow`'s layer 0, and `Inside`/`Immersion` carry the visitor into it.
- **No video.** A video path existed for the woodland close-ups and was deleted on review: "drawings,
  not video (frame-exact, seekable, one colour pipeline)" and "a clock that tolerates more than a
  drawing's drift" (woodland record, the artist's shot list).
- **The phrase list is at the 120 cap.** "Almost" must open from a line that already exists.
  `your-hand` ("your hand, almost", north wall, faint, `still` tier) is the natural one.

## 2. Tools: the decision

| Option | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| **A. Blender, procedural miniature, headless `bpy` + Cycles** | **Chosen for the film** |
| B. Blender with painted texture maps | Taken in part: hand-authored procedural materials, no photo textures |
| C. Real-time 3D in the browser (Three.js) | Rejected for the film |
| D. Blender film presented by the existing Three.js memory system | **Chosen for integration** |
| E. Others (Blender MCP, video models, cloth simulation, hair curves, EEVEE) | Rejected or not needed, below |

**A, why.** The defining gesture is a hand crossing between two bodies to a fold of cloth, then a
startle, a retreat and a lean back. That needs real space. Real depth gives contact and
non-penetration, which can be checked by measurement. Physical light gives what makes a miniature
read as a miniature: a small bulb's fast fall-off over centimetres, soft contact shadows,
backlit fibres, and a real lens's shallow focus at 30 cm. Cycles on the CPU is slow but
deterministic: with a fixed seed, a held pose is the same photograph.

**How the film is made.**
- **Puppets.** Stuffed felt shapes with a wire-like armature: bones driven by IK targets for the
  arms, and FK for the spine and head.
- **Fibres.** Seeded particle hair grown from the deforming surface, so it moves with the felt and
  never boils between frames.
- **Cloth.** No simulation. The sleeve is shaped pose by pose with shape keys, as a puppet maker
  pins fabric between exposures.
- **The exposure sheet.** It is Python: the poses, and how many frames each is held, on twos by
  default, with holds and selected ones. It is keyed into the `.blend` with constant (stepped)
  interpolation, so the file opens as a real stop-motion timeline.
- **Rendering.** Only unique poses are rendered; a held frame is the same file again. The set
  never moves: it is rendered once as a plate, and each pose renders only the region that changes.

**C, why not.** Felt fibres, soft shadows and true focus in real time on a phone would look like the
"generic video-game diorama" the brief rules out, and a new real-time scene would break the
no-compile-after-boot rule.

**D, how.**
- **The film plays through the living path.** A small `FilmScene` takes LivingScene's place. It
  draws a muted, inline video into the memory's render target, so `MemoryWindow` and
  `Inside`/`Immersion` are reused as they are.
- **Why a video this time.** It reverses the earlier "no video", for reasons that don't apply to
  the woodland's close-ups:
  - A 30 s locked-off stop-motion film of about 200 unique 720p photographs would be 30–40 MB as
    still frames, against about 3–5 MB as video. Held frames compress to almost nothing.
  - The earlier objections are answered by construction. The video is slaved to the memory's one
    clock. Playing, it is corrected whenever it drifts by more than half a frame. Held, seeked or
    recorded, it is paused and seeked to the exact frame. Its sound is not in the video: the
    soundtrack is a separate `music:` cue on the memory's audio, so picture and sound share one
    master.
  - Colour: BT.709 tags, and a frame in the room checked against the render.
- **Formats.** WebM VP9, which the test Chromium plays, and MP4 H.264 for Safari.
- **Framing for upright phones.** One open-matte render feeds two crops: 16:9 for wide screens and
  9:16 for upright phones. On an upright phone, the 16:9 two-shot cropped to cover the screen would
  cut both figures in half.
- **The way in.** It uses `MemoryWindow`'s existing ordered reveal. A baked fibre order map says
  when each strand grows along its length and when the film's colour arrives, with a fibre layer in
  the `sketch` slot: data, not new shaders, if the look holds.

**E.**
- **Blender MCP: not used.**
  - Its add-on refuses background mode and needs a running GUI on localhost.
  - Its main power, arbitrary Python sent into Blender over a socket, is a code-execution
    surface with no benefit over scripts run headless.
  - The scripts are reproducible and reviewable.
- **Video generation models: not used.** They give no control of contact or of exact poses,
  flicker between frames, and would cost money.
- **Cloth simulation: not used**, for the reasons the brief gives.
- **Geometry Nodes hair curves: not needed.** Legacy particle hair already follows the armature,
  with less setup.
- **EEVEE: unavailable here.**
- **ClaudeAnimationBase and opus-video-skills** (evaluated for the woodland). Only their
  discipline is reused: deterministic frames, contact sheets, and inspecting frames rather than
  trusting code. Their cartoon styles are not.
- **GSAP and Theatre.js: already in the app.** The film's position is a Theatre key like the
  woodland's. No new dependency.
- **FFmpeg** encodes, crops and mixes.
- **Sound.**
  - Procedural (NumPy/SciPy) for the fridge, the room and the clock.
  - ElevenLabs sound-effect takes for the fabric, the wood and the chair, approved by the artist
    for this memory. They follow the existing ledger rules: a dry run first, a balance check before
    each batch, and `review: pending` until the artist listens.
  - Freesound needs a personal token to download originals, so it is not used unless a gap
    appears. Every source is documented.

## 3. Staging (before blocking)

- **Scale and set.** The scale is 1:6. The table top is 11.2 cm off the floor, the seats 7.1 cm, and
  the figures about 20 cm tall if standing.
- **Where they sit.** Side by side, behind the table, facing the lens. The initiator (A, rust
  sweater, rounder, softer shoulders) is on the left. The recipient (B, blue-grey cardigan, slimmer,
  more upright, a small bun) is on the right.
- **The sleeve.** B's right forearm rests on the table between them, its cuff turned back on itself
  so a wedge of ivory lining shows. A reaches with the near (left) hand.
- **The lap.** The table hides both laps from any lens near eye level. A hand returning to the lap
  is read from the shoulder dropping, the elbow coming in, and the hand leaving the table over its
  far edge. The table top is the shared ground, and the empty wood in front of A at the end is the
  absence. The blocking test checks whether this reads.
- **Lens.** A 40–50 mm lens at about 30 cm, stopped down so the cuff, the hands and both faces hold
  focus while the wall falls soft. The camera does not move.
- **Light.** One enamel pendant above the table is the key: a warm bulb, falling off fast. The cool
  window and the deep room fall away, and the light never changes to explain anything.
