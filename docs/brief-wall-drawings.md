# Brief: subtle wall drawings (October 2026)

A condensed copy of the artist's brief. It is the reference for review.

**Intention.** Small, light, emotionally resonant drawings on the walls. They are traces, not decoration and not illustrations: marks left by a person, or several people, made absentmindedly, emotionally, casually, privately, sometimes playfully, sometimes painfully, sometimes without explanation. They should make the room more human, more lived-in and more layered, without becoming a text-only installation. Think of emotional residue, memory fragments, domestic traces, childlike shorthand: marks made when words were not enough. They support the writing and never compete with it. The room stays a white room, one chair, restrained writing.

**Rules.**
- **Small.** Found, not displayed.
- **Light.** Pencil, faint graphite, soft charcoal grey, slightly smudged.
- **Incomplete or imperfect.** Hasty, partly erased, childlike, hesitant, uneven. Some are only fragments.
- **Restraint.** No covering the room in doodles.
- **No literalism.** A drawing may quietly echo a nearby phrase. Examples:
  - two cups by "I still say 'we' by accident.";
  - a childlike sun by "I think I was happy then.";
  - an unfinished face by "Do you remember me?".

  But not a caption for every sentence.

**Categories.**
- **Domestic memory**, the strongest: cups, spoon, bowl, crumbs, plate, couch, table, window, blanket, shoes, mug ring, lamp, cat, door.
- **Childlike**, used sparingly: sun, star, house, flower, cloud, stick figure, smiling face, cat, waves, heart, balloon.
- **Emotional-symbolic**, fewer than the others and not theatrical: eye, face without a mouth, lone chair, small door, two figures, crossed-out heart, a line between two people, an empty speech bubble.
- **Recurring motifs**: chair, two cups, sun, door, eye. They come back in different hands, at different levels of completion, partly erased or rewritten.

**Quantity.**
- About 8–14 in total, with 2–4 visible at the beginning.
- Uneven across walls; some walls nearly empty.
- Keep negative space around the chair, the spoken-line areas and important writing.

**Placement.**
- Feels discovered: beside or below writing, low, near corners, near erased writing, in quiet zones, at a casual hand height, sometimes too high or too low.
- Never centred, symmetrical, evenly spaced or gallery-like.

**Styles.**
1. Faint pencil doodle.
2. Childlike sketch: restrained, not cute.
3. Half-erased ghost.
4. Firmer casual sketch, for domestic objects and motifs.

All of them in the same material as the writing.

**Integration.**
- Extend the writing system (`WallText`, `layout`, `textures`).
- Drawings belong to the wall surfaces: no floating sprites, no stickers.

**Static and dynamic.**
- Some are there when the room appears.
- Some appear over time: sketched in, emerging, answering voice, attention or emotional state, or appearing beside a phrase after it is spoken. Use these sparingly.

**Spoken lines.** Selective links, for example:
- crumbs → crumbs or a plate;
- "laugh so hard" → a small smiling face;
- "stay" → a small chair or door, only if subtle.

**Animation.** Sketching in like invisible handwriting, or faint emergence. No typewriter tracing, glow, hologram or particles.

**Erosion.** Some partially erase, fade or smudge, or remain as ghosts.

**Performance.** Efficient on mobile: texture generation, material reuse, reveal cost, memory, overdraw. Important text must stay readable.

**Avoid.** A sketchbook explosion, large illustrations, cuteness, overused dark symbolism, a drawing for every phrase, a gallery of concept art, overpowering the text or the chair, icons or decals, losing the minimalism.

**Review criteria.**
- More human?
- Traces, not decoration?
- Deepen ambiguity rather than explain?
- Restrained?
- Memory and presence?
- Negative space kept?
- Physically integrated?
- No clutter?
- Readable on mobile?
- Still serious and intentional?

**Mandate.** "The kind of things someone leaves behind when they are alone, waiting, remembering, avoiding, or unable to say exactly what they mean. Personal. Not decorative. Not explanatory." The room is still mostly empty.

Also requested in the same message:
- a faint floor shadow under the writing in the air;
- a breath or eraser sound when it is rubbed out (eraser SFX plus the existing breath; the artist approved about 70 credits for three takes).
