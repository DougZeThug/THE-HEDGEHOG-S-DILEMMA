Every night, a fox climbed a hill to sit beneath the moon.

He told her small things: where the blackberries grew, what he had mistaken for a bird, which paths he disliked walking alone.

The moon began waiting for him.

From so far away, each found the other easy to love.

One evening, she asked him to meet her beside a pond.

There, she could lay her reflection close enough for him to rest his head beside it.

For many nights, they were happy.

She lit the paths he had been afraid to walk. He told her stories about the world beneath the trees, where she had never been.

But there were things the fox did not like anyone to see.

When he was frightened, he hid. When he was ashamed, he pretended he had somewhere else to go.

The moon had her own difficulty. When someone grew quiet, she felt herself disappearing.

So when the fox withdrew beneath the reeds, she shone a little brighter.

She wanted him to know she was still there.

But now he could see the whole of himself in the water: his muddy coat, his trembling legs, the face he made when he was trying not to cry.

He asked for a little darkness.

She dimmed her light. For a while, they rested together.

But when he stayed quiet, she began to wonder whether he wanted her there at all.

Her light crept back across the pond.

The fox pulled a branch over the water.

He meant to make a hiding place.

From beneath the leaves, the moon watched her face disappear.

She rose away from the pond.

The fox pushed the branch aside and reached after her. His paw struck the water, breaking her reflection into pieces.

By the time the pond was still, she was high above the hill again.

For several nights, he slept without being seen.

For several nights, she crossed the sky without wondering whether she was welcome.

Neither was hurt again.

Neither returned to the pond.

Then, one evening, the fox climbed the hill with a small, unimportant story he had saved for her.

She was already looking down.

He began telling it.

Halfway through, he looked toward the pond.

The moon looked too.
